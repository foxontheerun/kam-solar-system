KAM stability

KAM (Kolmogorov–Arnold–Moser): a theorem about which orbits in a perturbed gravitational system stay stable. Orbits with irrational period ratios — especially the golden ratio φ — resist perturbations the longest; integer ratios like 2:1 and 3:1 collapse through resonance. The controls below let you push the system past these thresholds and watch what survives.

Distances are given in AU (astronomical units; 1 AU ≈ 149.6 million km, the mean Earth–Sun distance). Jupiter's real orbital distance is 5.20 AU; the presets below pull it inward to specific resonance positions with Earth.

Presets
  • Real (5.20 AU) — restores the actual Solar System. No perturbations.
  • φ — stable — Jupiter at a ≈ 1.376 AU; Jupiter:Earth period ratio = φ (golden ratio). KAM-protected, no collapse on visible timescales.
  • 2:1 → collapse — Jupiter at a ≈ 1.587 AU + masses ×50. Earth–Jupiter mean-motion resonance; inner system ejected within decades. Same mechanism that carves the Kirkwood gap at 3.27 AU in the real belt.
  • 3:1 → collapse — Jupiter at a ≈ 2.08 AU + masses ×30. Weaker MMR than 2:1, slightly slower collapse.
  • ⚠ break KAM — 2:1 position + masses ×800. Jupiter becomes gravitationally stellar-mass (0.76 M⊙); the Sun–Jupiter pair forms a tight binary. Earth and Mars fall inside Jupiter's stellar Hill sphere (R ≈ 1 AU); Mercury and Venus are ejected by the perturbation. Neptune at 30 AU survives and ends up on a circumbinary orbit.
  • ⟳ reset state — restore positions and velocities to the last saved snapshot.

Jupiter–Saturn TS/TJ = 2.484
Saturn:Jupiter period ratio, computed from osculating semi-major axes (`a` recovered from current position and velocity via vis-viva), so the value reacts to perturbations and to KAM-preset changes in real time. In the real Solar System this equals 2.484 ≈ 5:2 — the Great Inequality, identified by Halley (1695) and Euler (1750s) and quantitatively explained by Laplace in 1785 as a near-5:2 commensurability. It drives oscillations in Jupiter's and Saturn's semi-major axes (amplitudes ≈ 0.002 AU and 0.005 AU respectively) and mean longitudes over a libration period of ≈ 883 years.

About scales

Distances from the Sun are real in astronomical units (Mercury 0.387 AU, Neptune 30.07 AU).

Body sizes are exaggerated. Earth's actual diameter is 11,700× smaller than its orbital radius — on screen that would be 0.07 pixels. Use the «Visual scale» panel above to see how much they have been inflated.

Barycenter. In reality the centre of mass of the Sun–Jupiter system lies outside the Sun's surface (at 1.07 solar radii from the centre). With the Sun visually enlarged here, the marker appears inside the Sun's drawn body. Reduce the Sun's visual scale to see the real geometric relation.

Time is accelerated — the speed slider ranges from 1 to 1000 years/s.

Orbital lines are theoretical (Keplerian, two-body). Under strong perturbations planets deviate from them — that is the breakdown of KAM tori.

Asteroid belt is decorative — particles follow fixed Kepler orbits around the Sun's mesh, do not participate in N-body gravity, and rigidly track the Sun's wobble in the inertial frame (rather than orbiting the barycenter independently). It auto-hides when a planet falls into the Sun, so the belt doesn't keep peacefully orbiting through a collapsed system.
